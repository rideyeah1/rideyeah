Airport Overview

Los Angeles International Airport (LAX) is the busiest airport on the U.S. West Coast and one of the busiest in the world, handling tens of millions of passengers each year. It sits about 18 miles southwest of Downtown Los Angeles, close to the coast in the Westchester neighborhood.

LAX has nine numbered passenger terminals arranged in the iconic U-shaped Central Terminal Area (CTA), connected airside and by a free shuttle landside. The airport is in the middle of a major modernization: the Automated People Mover (APM) train, a consolidated car rental facility, and a full rebuild of Terminals 4–5 — Terminal 5 closed in late 2025, so several airlines have changed terminals recently.

Because of its size and persistent traffic in the horseshoe, LAX rewards travelers who plan ahead — knowing your terminal, where rideshare pickups happen, and how much time to budget makes a big difference.

Serves: Los Angeles, Santa Monica, Beverly Hills, Hollywood, Downtown LA, South Bay

Rideshare, Pickup & Drop-off

Rideshare and app-based pickups at LAX are handled at LAX-it (pronounced “LAX-it”), a dedicated pickup lot just east of Terminal 1. After landing, follow signs to LAX-it — you can walk (about 5–15 minutes depending on terminal) or take the free, frequent LAX-it shuttle that loops the terminals.

Pickup zones

  • LAX-it lot, east of Terminal 1 — all app-based rideshare pickups
  • Free LAX-it shuttle stops are located on the lower (arrivals) level at each terminal

Drop-off

Departing passengers are dropped off on the upper (departures) level directly in front of each terminal. Drop-off is not restricted to LAX-it — only pickups use the lot.

LAX security lines and terminal-to-gate walks can be long. Add time during holidays, early-morning banks of departures, and if you need to check bags.

Tips for Travelers

  • Confirm your terminal before you leave — airline-to-terminal assignments at LAX have shifted in recent years.
  • If connecting between terminals, most are connected airside post-security, but some transfers require leaving and re-clearing security. Check your itinerary.
  • Traffic in the horseshoe (Central Terminal Area) backs up at peak times; an experienced driver can save you significant time.
  • For international arrivals, allow extra time for immigration and customs at Tom Bradley before any onward connection.
  • Free Wi-Fi is available throughout the terminals.
